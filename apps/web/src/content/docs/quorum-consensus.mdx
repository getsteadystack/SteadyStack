The core architectural innovation in SteadyStack is **Quorum Consensus Verification**.

---

## The Problem with Legacy Monitors

Single-origin uptime monitors query your endpoints from one static IP or datacenter. When a Tier 1 telecom transit route experiences localized BGP flapping, high packet loss, or regional DDoS scrubbing, legacy tools assume your entire application is down and page your on-call engineers.

A single monitoring node sees a dropped packet, a slow TLS handshake, or a momentary route flap — and triggers a 3am page. Your service was fine the entire time. Do that repeatedly and teams experience alert fatigue, which is worse than having no monitoring at all.

---

## The Four-Step Verification Protocol

SteadyStack never alerts based on a single region's observation. Every synthetic check and outage detection follows a strict four-step verification lifecycle:

### Step 1: Breadth (Multi-Region Probing)

Every monitor is checked across global edge regions (7 regions on paid tiers, 3 primary regions on free tier): Western and Eastern North America, Western and Eastern Europe, and three Asia-Pacific regions. Checks execute concurrently rather than as a rotating single-node scan.

### Step 2: Verify (Immediate Local Re-Check)

When a probe observes a non-2xx response or timeout, it does not immediately dispatch an incident. The region that observed the failure immediately executes a secondary re-check. Single dropped packets and transient TLS connection timeouts are filtered out at this stage without alerting on-call personnel.

### Step 3: Confirm (Quorum Consensus)

If the local re-check confirms the failure, the central orchestrator queries partner edge nodes across global edge regions:

- **Free Tier**: Requires **2 of 3 primary edge regions** to confirm the failure.
- **Paid Tiers**: Requires **4 of 7 global edge regions** to independently confirm failure.

A region that is slow to respond or timing out on network transit is treated separately to prevent network congestion from skewing the quorum.

### Step 4: Classify (Outage vs. Regional Degradation)

Before dispatching an alert, SteadyStack classifies the failure mode:

- **Regional Degradation**: When a subset of regions fail (e.g. 3 failing while 4 succeed), the incident is classified as regional degradation (often caused by localized CDN edge failure or ISP routing issues).
- **Global Outage**: When quorum threshold is met across majority regions, the incident is classified as a confirmed global outage.

## Worked Verification Examples

### Worked Example A: Regional Degradation (No Global Page)

Consider a scenario where transatlantic transit routes flap, causing US and Western Europe probes to fail while other regions succeed:

```text
Region Results:
├── wnam (US West)     -> FAIL (504 Gateway Timeout)
├── enam (US East)     -> FAIL (504 Gateway Timeout)
├── weur (EU West)     -> FAIL (504 Gateway Timeout)
├── eeur (EU East)     -> OK (200 OK, 42ms)
├── apac (Tokyo)       -> OK (200 OK, 110ms)
├── apac-ne (Seoul)    -> TIMEOUT (Excluded from quorum)
└── apac-se (Singapore)-> OK (200 OK, 88ms)

Consensus: 3 of 6 counted regions report failure.
Threshold: 4 of 7 required.
Result: Quorum not met. No 3am on-call page. Logged to dashboard as Regional Degradation.
```

### Worked Example B: Confirmed Global Outage (Incident Dispatched)

Consider a primary database failover or upstream application crash:

```text
Region Results:
├── wnam (US West)     -> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)
├── enam (US East)     -> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)
├── weur (EU West)     -> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)
├── eeur (EU East)     -> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)
├── apac (Tokyo)       -> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)
├── apac-ne (Seoul)    -> OK (Cached response)
└── apac-se (Singapore)-> FAIL (500 Internal Server Error)

Consensus: 6 of 7 regions confirm failure.
Threshold: 4 of 7 met.
Result: Quorum achieved. Critical incident opened, on-call paged via PagerDuty/Slack, status page updated.
```

## Regional Coverage & Transparency

SteadyStack deploys edge probes across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific (including Tokyo and Sydney). Regions with active edge probes are published with live health telemetry. For endpoints serving users outside these primary zones, pairing multi-region checks with heartbeat and cron-job monitoring ensures complete coverage.
